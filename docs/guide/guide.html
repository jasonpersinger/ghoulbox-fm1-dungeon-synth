Welcome to the dungeon

GHOULBOX turns the M-VAVE FM-1 into a dungeon-synth instrument: hurdy-gurdies and drones, crypt pads and monk choirs, organs, harps and recorders, all through a cathedral reverb and a worn cassette.

It is a fork of Felucca, the open-source FM-1 firmware by Hügelton Instruments. Everything Felucca does still works here: four tracks, a 64-step sequencer, songs, the arpeggiator, chord keys, effects, the web editor and backups. GHOULBOX adds the sounds, the reverb and tape, a hurdy-gurdy engine, dungeon drums and an old-RPG look, and leaves out the sounds that don't fit the genre.

How to use this guide

  • New to synthesizers? Read Part I to get the FM-1 running, then work through Part II. Each chapter there ends in a Try this box: a few steps on the instrument that make the idea audible. Nothing you do in them can break anything, and holding SAVE undoes the last sound or pattern load.
  • Know synths already? Part III is the reference: every page, every sound, every setting.
  • Want to make a piece? Part IV walks through one from an empty track to a recording, with a chord cheat sheet.

Buttons and knobs are written like PLAY. A path such as SEQ › STEP means "press SEQ until the STEP page shows".

1 · Meet the FM-1

The FM-1 is a small battery-powered synthesizer: a keyboard, a screen, eight knobs, a speaker and a computer chip that makes the sound. GHOULBOX is the program that runs on that chip.

M-VAVE sells the FM-1 with its own firmware, an FM synthesizer. GHOULBOX replaces that program completely, so most of M-VAVE's manual (its pages, presets and buttons) does not apply. The hardware is the same, though, and this chapter covers it.

On the top

  • 27 keys (silicone, about two and a quarter octaves), each with a light under it. They are not velocity-sensitive: every key plays at the same strength. A MIDI keyboard can add velocity (chapter 20).
  • MASTER, the volume knob. It has a start and an end, like a volume knob on a radio.
  • Seven endless knobs: SELECT, PRESETS, ALGORITHM and KNOB 1–4. They turn forever in either direction and move a value in clicks.
  • Buttons for pages, transport (PLAY, REC) and octave (OCT−, OCT+).
  • A 1.54-inch colour screen.
  • A speaker, small but enough to practise with.

On the back

SocketWhat it's for
Audio out (3.5 mm)Headphones, or a cable to speakers, a mixer or an audio interface. Plugging something in silences the speaker. Use a normal stereo (TRS, two black rings) plug: a phone-headset plug with three rings can buzz and may not silence the speaker.
MIDI IN (3.5 mm)A cable from another instrument's MIDI OUT, through a small 3.5 mm-to-MIDI adapter. M-VAVE's manual doesn't say which of the two adapter wirings (called Type A and Type B) it uses; if a keyboard plays nothing through it, try the other type.
USB-CCharging, and the connection to a computer: MIDI both ways, the FM-1's sound as a USB audio input, and the installer and editor.
Power switchOn and off.

Power and battery

  • A built-in 2000 mAh battery. M-VAVE quotes about 12 hours of play; that figure is for its own firmware, and GHOULBOX's reverb works the chip harder.
  • Charge it from any USB-C charger or computer port. It also runs from USB power.
  • The battery level is in the top-right corner of the screen.

No Bluetooth

M-VAVE's firmware offers wireless (Bluetooth) MIDI. Felucca and GHOULBOX don't: use USB or the MIDI IN socket instead.

Specifications

Size161.5 × 96.5 × 28.6 mm
Weight251 g
Battery3.7 V lithium-polymer, 2000 mAh, charged over USB-C
Sound under GHOULBOX44.1 kHz stereo; four tracks sharing 8 voices
Look after it as you would a phone: keep it dry, don't leave it in a hot car, and charge it now and then if it sits unused for months, so the battery never runs completely flat.

2 · Install, back up, go back

What you need

  • A computer with Chrome or Edge (other browsers can't talk MIDI to the FM-1)
  • A USB data cable (some cables only charge)
  • No other music software open (it can hold on to the FM-1's MIDI port)

Install

  1. Open jasonpersinger.github.io/ghoulbox-fm1-dungeon-synth (it opens the installer at its https address).
  2. Connect the FM-1, switch it on, and press Install. Allow MIDI access when the browser asks.
  3. The installer saves a backup of your music, sounds and settings first, then switches the FM-1 to update mode and writes GHOULBOX.
  4. When it says done, the FM-1 restarts with the GHOULBOX skull. Hold HOME › SYSTEM › ABOUT shows the version.
If the installer stops after it says "update mode", unplug the USB cable, plug it back in, and press Install again. The FM-1 stays in update mode until the install is finished, so nothing is lost.

Updating later

Run the installer again. It backs up first each time, and your projects and sounds stay where they are.

Going back

To return to Felucca, install it from hugelton.github.io/Felucca and restore your backup in its editor. Its Return to official V15 button puts back M-VAVE's own firmware.

Your old music

Projects and user presets from Felucca load in GHOULBOX. Sounds GHOULBOX no longer lists (for example the techno presets) still load and play inside an old project; they are only missing from the menus.

3 · Five-minute start

GHOULBOX powers on ready to play a small dungeon piece.

  1. Press PLAY. Four tracks play a scene in D minor at 72 BPM: a hurdy-gurdy melody (track 1), a crypt pad (2), chanting monks (3) and a drone (4), through the hall reverb. Press PLAY again to stop.
  2. Turn MASTER for volume. Headphones in the jack silence the speaker.
  3. Turn ALGORITHM to choose the track (T1–T4). Everything you do applies to that track.
  4. Turn PRESETS to change the track's sound while it plays. Your pattern stays.
  5. Play the keys to play the selected track. OCT− / OCT+ shift the octave.
  6. Turn SELECT to change the tempo.
  7. Turn KNOB 1–4 to shape the sound: on HOME they are the engine's four main controls.
Made a mess? Hold SAVE to undo the last sound or pattern load. Switching the FM-1 off and on brings back the power-on scene (your saved projects are kept).

4 · The panel

The FM-1's controls, as Felucca (and GHOULBOX) use them. The labels printed on the panel are M-VAVE's; most still fit, and the diagram shows what each one does now.

Panel diagram from Felucca's documentation (Hügelton Instruments, GPL-3.0). The screen in it shows Felucca's look; GHOULBOX's is in the next chapter.

Three ways to press a button

  • Tap a page button for its page, and again for the next page in its group (chapter 14 lists them all). HOME returns to the home screen.
  • Hold FX, GLO, SCL or EDIT for a quick layer: shortcuts on the keys and knobs, with a map on the screen. Let go to close it.
  • Double-tap one of those four to keep its layer open without holding; tap it again to close.

Other holds: HOME opens the menu, SAVE undoes, SEQ opens the song page, and REC on a sequencer page clears the track's steps (it asks first). In the EDIT layer, each white key picks an engine.

Yes and no

Wherever the FM-1 asks something (a dialog, an action page), OCT+ means yes / do it and OCT− means no / go back. Their lights show when they answer.

5 · The screen

GHOULBOX draws Felucca's pages as an old role-playing game would: stone windows, a pixel font, flickering wall torches beside the scope, and a sword that points at what is selected.

  • The header shows play state, tempo, the track number and battery.
  • Knob cards: most pages show four cards in a row, one for each of KNOB 1–4, left to right. Each has a name, a value and a small gauge. The one you just turned is drawn in the accent colour.
  • The scope on HOME draws the sound itself as a moving line: smooth for soft sounds, jagged for bright ones.
  • Messages (for example SOUND INIT, STOP TO SAVE or NO SAMPLE) appear in the header for a moment.
  • Colours: a new FM-1 starts in CRYPT (stone, bone, torch amber, blood red). If you came from Felucca, your saved palette stays: hold HOME and pick COLOR › CRYPT.

The lights

Idle buttons and keys glow dimly so you can find them in the dark. A button that does something right now breathes slowly. PLAY turns green while playing, and the keys light up with the notes being played, whether by you, the sequencer or MIDI. MENU › LEDS sets how bright the idle glow is.

6 · What a synthesizer does

Every sound is air shaking back and forth. A synthesizer is a machine that decides exactly how it shakes.

Any sound you hear differs from any other in three ways:

  • Pitch: how high or low. A fast shake is a high note, a slow one a low note.
  • Loudness, and how it changes: a harp note starts loud and fades; an organ note stays the same while held.
  • Tone (or timbre): what makes a flute and a trumpet sound different on the same note. Bright sounds carry many high overtones; dark, soft sounds carry few.

A synthesizer has a part for each of these. Most of them, including the FM-1, are built the same way:

OSCILLATOR → FILTER → AMPLIFIER → EFFECTS
  • The oscillator makes the raw sound at the pitch of the key.
  • The filter shapes the tone, usually by removing brightness.
  • The amplifier sets the loudness over time, shaped by an envelope.
  • Effects place the sound in a space: reverb, delay, chorus, tape.

On top of that sit modulators, which move the other parts by themselves (a slow wobble, a swell), and a sequencer, which plays notes for you.

Engines

The FM-1 under GHOULBOX has 13 engines: thirteen different ways of making the raw sound. ANALOG copies the classic synthesizers of the 1970s and 80s; VOICE sings vowels; GURDY models a hurdy-gurdy; SAMPLE plays recordings of real instruments. Each engine comes with ready-made settings called presets (or sounds), 59 of them in all.

Tracks

GHOULBOX has four tracks, like four musicians. Each has its own engine and sound, its own notes in the sequencer, and its own settings. The ALGORITHM knob chooses which track you are playing and editing; the screen shows T1–T4. All four share 8 voices (8 notes sounding at once), so a big chord on one track can cut short a long note on another.

TRY THIS · Hear the parts
  1. Press PLAY. Turn ALGORITHM to T2 (the pad).
  2. Hold GLO to open its layer. While holding it, the first four black keys (from F♯ in the lower octave) mute and unmute tracks 1–4; their lights show which are sounding. Mute all but track 2 and listen to the pad alone, then bring the others back one at a time.
  3. With T2 still selected, turn PRESETS slowly: the pad's part plays on a different sound each time, while the notes stay the same. Hold SAVE to undo the last change.

7 · Notes, keys and scales

You don't need to read music to play the FM-1. A few facts about the keyboard go a long way.

The keyboard

The keys repeat a pattern of 7 white and 5 black keys. Each group of 12 is an octave: the same note names again, twice as high. The white keys are named A to G; the white key just left of each pair of two black keys is C. Each key is one semitone above the key to its left, black or white.

OCT− and OCT+ move the whole keyboard down or up an octave.

Don't hold OCT− and OCT+ down together: that starts a countdown to update mode, which the installer uses. If UPDATE MODE IN appears on the screen, let go: it says UPDATE CANCELLED and nothing has changed. If you hold on until the screen says UBOOT, the FM-1 is waiting for new firmware: connect it to the computer and run the installer again.

Scales and keys

A scale is a choice of 7 of the 12 notes that sound good together. A piece "in D minor" uses mostly the notes of the D minor scale, starts and ends on D, and feels like home on D. Minor scales sound sad, dark or ancient; that's why most dungeon synth is in minor.

The root is the home note (D in D minor). Every scale has a different mood:

Scale (SCL)Mood
MIN (natural minor)Sad, plain, the default of dungeon synth
DOR (Dorian)Minor but hopeful; folk tunes, wandering
PHRY (Phrygian)Dark and tense, the note above the root a little too close
HARM (harmonic minor)Old-world, dramatic, "castle" sounding
MPEN (minor pentatonic)Five notes, nothing clashes: easy to improvise with
MAJ (major)Bright, happy: a tavern, a sunrise

Let the FM-1 play only the right notes

On SCL, set ROOT (KNOB 1) and SCL (KNOB 2), then QNT (KNOB 3) to WHITE. Now the white keys play only the notes of your scale, starting on the root at the C key, and the black keys are silent. You can't hit a wrong note.

QNT SNAP keeps every key but moves a note that isn't in the scale down to the nearest one that is. QNT OFF is the plain keyboard.

TRY THIS · D minor on the white keys
  1. Stop playback. Choose T1 with ALGORITHM.
  2. Tap SCL. Turn KNOB 1 to ROOT D, KNOB 2 to SCL MIN, KNOB 3 to QNT WHITE.
  3. Play the white keys from C upwards: you hear the D minor scale. Play a slow, simple tune; finish it on the C key (now D) and hear it come home.
  4. Change SCL to PHRY and play the same tune: darker. Try HARM: suddenly a castle.

The scale settings belong to the track, so set them on each track you play from the keys. Chords and the scale cheat sheet are in chapter 23.

8 · Tone: oscillators and the filter

The oscillator makes the raw sound; the filter carves it.

Waveforms

The oscillator repeats one shape, the waveform, over and over. Its shape decides the raw tone:

WaveSounds like
SIN (sine)Pure and round, almost a whistle. No overtones.
TRI (triangle)Soft and hollow: a recorder or a flute.
SQR (square)Hollow and reedy: a clarinet, old video games.
SAW (sawtooth)Bright and buzzy, full of overtones: strings, brass. The usual start for pads.
PWM (pulse width)A square that keeps changing its shape: rich and moving.

Two oscillators slightly out of tune with each other (detune) beat against each other and sound wider and warmer, like several players at once.

The filter

The filter removes part of the sound. The usual one is a low-pass filter: it lets the low part through and takes away the bright part above a point called the cutoff.

  • CUT (cutoff): turn it down and the sound gets darker, softer and further away; up, brighter and closer.
  • RES (resonance): makes the filter ring at the cutoff, adding a whistling, vocal edge. High values sound "synthy".
  • KTR (key tracking): how much the cutoff follows the notes, so high notes don't sound duller than low ones.

Not every engine is built this way. GURDY, VOICE, PHYS and the others have their own controls for tone (chapter 15), but the idea holds: some control makes the sound brighter or darker.

TRY THIS · Open the filter
  1. On T2, turn PRESETS to CRYPT PAD (an ANALOG sound) and go to HOME.
  2. On HOME, KNOB 1 is CUT and KNOB 2 is RES. Hold a chord with one hand and slowly turn KNOB 1 down, then up: the pad closes and opens like a door.
  3. Turn RES up halfway and sweep CUT again: hear the whistle ride along.
  4. Tap EDIT for all eight ANALOG controls. Change WAVE (KNOB 1) between SAW, SQR, TRI and SIN and hear the raw tone change. Hold SAVE to undo if you get lost.

9 · Shape in time: the envelope

An envelope describes how a note changes from the moment you press the key until it falls silent.

The FM-1's envelope has four stages, called ADSR:

StageWhat it setsLowHigh
ATK attackHow long the note takes to reach full loudnessInstant: a pluck, a hitA slow swell: strings, choirs
DEC decayHow long it takes to fall from the peak to the sustain levelA quick dropA slow settle
SUS sustainThe loudness while the key stays held (a level, not a time)Fades to silence: a harp, a bellHolds: an organ, a pad
REL releaseHow long the note rings after you let goStops deadFades out slowly into the reverb

A pluck (harp, lute): attack 0, short decay, sustain 0. A pad (strings, choir): long attack, full sustain, long release. An organ: attack 0, sustain full, short release.

The envelope does more than loudness

Tap ENV again for ENV DEST: how much the same envelope also moves the filter (FLT), the pitch (PIT) and the engine's shape control (SHP). Envelope on the filter makes a note start bright and darken as it fades, as real plucked strings do.

TRY THIS · Turn a pad into a harp
  1. Keep CRYPT PAD on T2. On HOME, KNOB 3 is attack and KNOB 4 is release; or tap ENV for all four.
  2. On ENV, turn ATK (KNOB 1) to zero, DEC (KNOB 2) to about a third, SUS (KNOB 3) to zero. Play a few notes: the pad is now a pluck.
  3. Turn REL (KNOB 4) up and hear each pluck ring on after you let go.
  4. Now the other way: ATK up high, SUS full. Each note swells in slowly, like a choir taking a breath.

10 · Movement: the LFO and modulation

A sound that never changes sounds like a machine. Modulation makes it move.

The LFO

An LFO (low-frequency oscillator) is an oscillator too slow to hear. Instead of making sound, it moves a control up and down for you, smoothly and endlessly.

  • LFO page: RATE (how fast), WAVE (the shape of the movement: SIN and TRI glide smoothly, SQR jumps between two values, SAW ramps, S&H jumps to random values), PHS (where in its cycle each note starts) and FADE (how long it waits to come in after a note starts).
  • LFO again for LFO DEST: how much it moves the pitch (PIT), the filter (FLT), the shape (SHP) and the loudness (AMP).
LFO to…Is calledSounds like
Pitch, slow and smallVibratoA singer or violinist holding a note
LoudnessTremoloA shimmering organ, a pulsing pad
FilterA sweep, a "wah"The sound breathing open and closed

The modulation matrix

Tap LFO a third time for MOD: four slots, each connecting a source to a destination with an amount (AMT, which can be negative to turn the movement upside down). KNOB 1 chooses the slot.

  • Sources: LFO, ENV (the envelope), VEL (how hard a MIDI key is struck), KEY (how high the note is), RAND (a new random value each note), and from a MIDI controller MODW (the mod wheel), AT (aftertouch) and EXPR (an expression pedal).
  • Destinations: pitch, cutoff, shape, loudness, pan, the four effect sends, the LFO's own rate, vibrato, and the engine's eight controls (E1–E8).
TRY THIS · Make it breathe
  1. On T2 with CRYPT PAD, tap LFO: RATE to about a quarter, WAVE to TRI.
  2. Tap LFO again (LFO DEST). Turn FLT (KNOB 2) up slowly while holding a chord: the pad starts to swell and fade by itself.
  3. Set FLT back to 0 and turn PIT (KNOB 1) up only a little: vibrato. Turn RATE up: a nervous warble. Further: a broken tape.
  4. On MOD, set slot 1 to SRC RAND, DST CUT, a small AMT: each note comes out a little different, as if played by a person.

11 · Voices: poly, mono and glide

How many notes a track plays at once, and how it moves between them.

Tap EDIT until the VOICE page shows. Its first knob, VCE, has four settings:

VCEWhat it doesGood for
POLYSeveral notes at once: chordsPads, organs, harps
MONOOne note at a time; a new key cuts the old noteBasses, lead melodies, the hurdy-gurdy
LEG (legato)One note; if keys overlap, the note slides on without starting againSmooth, singing melodies; flutes and voices
UNI (unison)One note played by several voices, detuned against each otherHuge, wide leads and drones
  • GLD (glide, also called portamento): the pitch slides from one note to the next instead of jumping. A little sounds like a voice; a lot sounds like a siren.
  • GLMOD: RATE (big jumps take longer to slide) or TIME (every slide takes the same time).
  • PRIO (mono only): when several keys are held, which one plays: the LAST pressed, the LOW one or the HIGH one.
  • VOICE 2 (tap EDIT again): DTUNE (how far apart the unison voices are), PAN (where the track sits from left to right) and MUTE.
The four tracks share 8 voices in all. A UNI track uses several voices for each note, so with long chords on other tracks, some notes may be cut short. If notes drop out, use MONO where a part only needs one note at a time.
TRY THIS · A singing lead
  1. Choose T1 (the hurdy-gurdy) and turn PRESETS to LOFI FLUTE or TENOR RECORD.
  2. Tap EDIT until VOICE shows. Set VCE to LEG and GLD to about a fifth.
  3. Play a slow tune and let the keys overlap a little: the notes slide into each other. Lift between notes and they start fresh.
  4. Try VCE UNI on CRYPT PAD with one finger: one key, a wall of sound.

12 · Space: effects

Effects put the instrument in a room. In dungeon synth, the room is half the music.

EffectWhat it does
ReverbThe echoes of a room, all blurred together: a closet, a chapel, a cathedral. GHOULBOX's HALL rings for up to 12 seconds.
DelayDistinct repeats of what you play, each quieter than the last: a call across a valley.
ChorusA slightly wavering copy of the sound mixed in, as if several players: wider and shimmering.
DistortionOverdrives the sound until it breaks up: grit, growl.
TapeThe whole mix through an old cassette: wavering pitch, warmth, dull treble and hiss.
CrushA cheap old sampler: rougher, grainier, robotic at the extreme.

Sends: one room, four players

There is one reverb, one delay and one chorus, shared by all four tracks, just as four musicians share one hall. Each track decides how much of itself to send into each: tap FX, and KNOB 1–4 are the track's distortion, chorus, delay and reverb amounts. A pad drowned in reverb and a dry, close melody in front of it is a classic dungeon-synth picture.

Tap FX again to reach the shared effects themselves: SLICER, DLY, REVERB, CHORUS and TAPE (chapter 16).

TRY THIS · From closet to cathedral
  1. Play the scene. Tap FX until REVERB shows. Turn SIZE (KNOB 2) all the way down, then slowly all the way up.
  2. Turn DAMP (KNOB 3) up: the echoes go dark, like stone. Down: bright, like glass.
  3. Tap FX again until TAPE. Turn TAPE (KNOB 1) up to 100 and listen to the pitch waver. Add CRSH (KNOB 2) slowly: grit, then crunch.
  4. Back on the first FX page, choose T1 and turn its REV send (KNOB 4) to zero: the melody steps out of the hall and stands in front of you.

13 · Time: tempo, steps and the arpeggiator

Rhythm on the FM-1 comes from a clock that every track follows.

Tempo

The tempo is measured in BPM, beats per minute: 60 is one beat a second. Dungeon synth usually sits slow, 50 to 90. Turn SELECT to change it.

Steps

A step sequencer divides time into equal boxes, steps, and plays what is in each box in turn, round and round. Each track has its own row of up to 64 steps. Two settings on SEQ › PATTERN decide how it moves:

  • LEN: how many steps before it goes round again.
  • DIV: how long each step is, as a fraction of a bar (a bar is four beats). 1/4 is one beat per step, 1/8 half a beat, 1/1 a whole bar. The power-on scene uses 1/8.

LEN 16 at 1/8 is two bars; LEN 4 at 1/1 is four bars, one chord each. Each track can have a different length, so parts drift against each other: a 7-step melody over an 8-step bass never repeats the same way twice in a row.

Swing (SWG) delays every second step a little: a lilt, a dance, a limp.

The arpeggiator

An arpeggio is a chord played one note at a time, as a harpist does. The arpeggiator does it for you: hold a chord, and it plays its notes in turn, in time with the tempo. Tap ARP:

  • MODE: OFF, UP, DN (down), UPDN (up and down), RND (random), ORD (the order you pressed the keys), REPEAT (the whole chord again and again).
  • RATE: how fast, as a note length. OCT: how many octaves it climbs (1–4). GATE: how long each note sounds, from short and plucked to tied together.
  • ARP 2: SWG (swing), PROB (a chance each note plays, for a sparser, human pattern), HOLD (keeps playing after you let go: hands free) and ORD.
TRY THIS · A harp that plays itself
  1. Choose T1. Turn PRESETS to FOLK HARP. Set its scale to D minor on WHITE as in chapter 7.
  2. Tap ARP: MODE UPDN, RATE 1/8, OCT 2.
  3. Hold the C, E and G keys (a D minor chord in WHITE mode): the harp rolls up and down. Move the same three-finger shape to A, C, E (B♭ major) and back.
  4. Tap ARP again and turn HOLD on: lift your hand and the harp keeps going. Turn SELECT and hear it follow the tempo.

Real recordings (SAMPLE)

The SAMPLE engine plays four real instruments, recorded by Versilian Studios and released into the public domain (CC0): a bowed psaltery (a medieval zither played with a bow), a Renaissance chamber organ, a baroque tenor recorder and a folk harp. Held notes on the first three sustain for as long as you hold the key. SAMPLE's SET knob lists them first, then your own samples (USR1–USR3, uploaded or recorded in the web editor). An empty user slot plays a plain tone and the screen says NO SAMPLE.

The hurdy-gurdy (GURDY)

A hurdy-gurdy is a medieval string instrument played by turning a crank: a rosined wheel rubs the strings, keys on the side stop the melody string, and other strings drone on without stopping. One of them, the trompette, sits on a loose bridge that buzzes when the player jerks the crank, giving the dance its rhythm. GURDY plays one melody note at a time over its drones. Its eight controls (EDIT):

Drums (DRUM)

The DRUM engine's CRYPT kit is built for dungeon synth: a low frame drum, a rope snare, a hand slap, a shaker, jingles, a timpani, a wooden stick and a gong. Two sounds use it: CRYPT KIT and TOMB DRUMS (lower, longer and further back in the hall), both suggesting the TOMBBEAT pattern. KIT also offers Felucca's kits (STD, 80, 10, 66, 55, 77) if you want something less ancient.

The keys follow the General MIDI drum map, the same one drum machines and computer drum tracks use: C is the frame drum, D the rope snare, A the timpani. On a DRUM track, SEQ › STEP becomes a drum grid: the white keys are the steps of the current bar and the black keys choose the lane (the drum); KNOB 1–4 are STEP, LANE, HIT and ACC (accent).

16 · Effects

Each track sends to shared effects; the amounts are on the FX page (KNOB 1–4: distortion, chorus, delay, reverb). Tap FX again to reach the effects themselves. Distortion and the slicer belong to each track; the others are shared.

REVERB

  • TYPE (KNOB 1): ROOM, SPRING or HALL. GHOULBOX starts in HALL.
  • SIZE: from a 1.5-second stone chamber to a 12-second cathedral.
  • DAMP: darkens the tail.

TAPE

  • TAPE: the whole mix through a worn cassette: wow and flutter (wavering pitch), saturation, treble loss and hiss. GHOULBOX starts at 40.
  • CRSH: a cheap sampler: fewer bits and a lower sample rate. Off by default; a little adds grit, a lot turns to crunch.

DLY

TIME (as a note length, so it follows the tempo), FDBK (feedback: how many repeats; past 100 it builds up on itself, so ride it carefully), COLR (darker or brighter repeats) and MIX. With HALL selected, the delay is limited to 0.74 s, because the hall borrows part of its memory.

CHORUS

CRT (rate) and CDP (depth). Slow and deep for a seasick, warped-tape shimmer; fast and shallow for a thicker sound.

SLICER

Chops the track's sound in time with the tempo. GATE cuts it on and off in one of 16 rhythm patterns (a pulsing pad); STUT repeats short slices of it (a stutter). RATE sets how fine the chopping is, DEPTH how deep.

The FX layer

Hold FX to play performance effects from the keys (repeat, reverse, filter sweeps, tape stop, freeze, a harmonizer with octave shimmer) and mute tracks with the black keys. With MENU › FX LATCH on, they toggle instead of needing to be held. The screen shows which key does what.

17 · Sequencer, patterns and songs

Each track has its own sequence of up to 64 steps, with chords, ties, accents, slides, per-step chance and ratchets.

Write notes step by step

  1. Go to SEQ › STEP. The cursor is the step being edited (KNOB 1 or PRESETS moves it).
  2. Press a key: it is written into the step. Press several together for a chord (up to 4 notes on a POLY track). When you let go of all keys, the cursor moves to the next step.
  3. Tap EDIT to empty the step and move on: a rest.
  4. Set TIME (KNOB 3) to TIE to hold the previous note through this step instead of playing a new one.
  5. FLAG (KNOB 4): ACC plays the step louder, SLD slides into the next note, A+S both.

Record live

Press REC to arm the selected track (from any page), press PLAY, and play: what you play is written into the steps as they pass. The sequence loops, and you can keep adding on each pass (overdub). Press REC again to stop recording. Knob moves on an armed track are recorded too (automation): the sound changes at those steps every time round. SEQ › AUTOMATION turns it off or clears it.

Chance and ratchets

On SEQ › CHANCE, give each step a CHANCE (the percentage of times it plays: lower values make a pattern sparse and alive) and a RATCH count (the step played 2, 3 or 4 times within its length: a drum roll).

Length, speed and clearing

  • SEQ › PATTERN: LEN (1–64 steps), DIV (step length, from 4 bars to 1/32), SWG (swing), GATE (how long notes sound within their step).
  • Clear the track's steps: hold REC on STEP, PATTERN or CHANCE (or SAVE › TOOLS › PAT); OCT+ clears, OCT− cancels. Holding SAVE undoes it.

Songs

A song chains up to four sections into a longer piece. Each section is a saved project: song pattern A is the steps of project 1, B of project 2, C of 3 and D of 4. The song plays them with the sounds currently loaded.

  1. Make a section and save it as project 1 (chapter 18). Change the notes for the next section and save it as project 2, and so on.
  2. Hold SEQ for the SONG page. KNOB 1 picks a row (up to 16); KNOB 2 sets which section it plays (A–D), KNOB 3 how many times (1–16). Turning KNOB 2 or 3 on the empty row after the last adds a new row.
  3. Press PLAY on the SONG page to play the song from the top. Edit the sections while stopped.

For example, rows A ×2, B ×1, A ×2, C ×1 give a verse, a bridge, the verse again and an ending. SAVE › TOOLS › ROW deletes a row and SONG clears the whole song.

18 · Saving your work

The FM-1 keeps two kinds of things: sounds (one track's sound) and projects (everything). Saving works only while stopped.

Save a project (4 slots)

A project keeps the whole session: all four tracks, their sounds and sequences, songs, tempo and effects.

  1. Stop. Tap SAVE until PROJECT shows.
  2. Choose the slot with KNOB 1. Turn KNOB 4 (SAVE) right to pick it, and press OCT+. If the slot already holds a project, it asks first: OCT+ again.
  3. Name it with the keys, and press OCT+ to save.

To load: same page, KNOB 3 (LOAD) right, then OCT+.

Save a sound (32 user slots)

  1. Stop and tap SAVE: it opens on USER.
  2. Pick a slot with KNOB 1, press OCT+, name it with the keys, and press OCT+ again.

Your sounds appear after the factory sounds when you turn PRESETS. On USER and PROJECT, EDIT renames the selected slot.

Undo

Hold SAVE to go back before the last sound load, pattern load or clear (hold again to redo).

Backups

The web editor (on the installer site) makes a full backup of the FM-1 to your computer and restores it. Do one now and then, and before any experiment you might regret.

The FM-1 always starts with the dungeon scene, not your last project. Your saved projects stay in their slots; load one from SAVE › PROJECT.

20 · MIDI, USB and recording

MIDI is the language electronic instruments use to talk to each other: which key went down, how hard, which knob moved. It carries no sound, only instructions.

What can talk to the FM-1

  • A computer over USB: a DAW (music software) can play the FM-1's tracks, and the FM-1's keys send notes back to it. No driver needed.
  • A MIDI keyboard or controller: over USB through a computer, or into the MIDI IN socket with a 3.5 mm adapter (chapter 1). A bigger keyboard gives you more octaves and touch-sensitive keys.
  • Another instrument's clock: GLO › GLOBAL › CLK set to USB or TRS makes the FM-1 follow the tempo and start/stop of a computer or drum machine.

Channels

MIDI has 16 channels, like 16 radio stations on one cable. With GLO › SYSTEM › ROUT on CH1-4, channels 1–4 play tracks 1–4 and 5–16 are ignored: a DAW can play all four tracks at once. With ROUT on SEL, every channel plays the selected track. Pitch bend and the sustain pedal work too.

Control changes (CC)

Knobs on a MIDI controller send control changes, numbered 0–127. These turn the FM-1's controls, on the channel's track:

CCControl
7 · 10Track level · pan
73 · 75 · 79 · 72Attack · decay · sustain · release
91 · 93 · 94Reverb · chorus · delay sends
21–24The four engine knobs (the HOME page's)
85 · 86CRSH · TAPE (the whole mix)
74The FX layer's filter on the whole mix: below 64 low-pass, above high-pass, 64 off (like the FX layer's own filter, it resets when the FX layer is let go or the menu opens)

On the selected track, while it is armed (REC), these moves are recorded as automation, as when turning its knob. The mod wheel, aftertouch and expression pedal are sources in the MOD matrix (chapter 10).

Recording to a computer

The FM-1 appears on a computer as a USB audio input named "Felucca", carrying the master output in stereo at 44.1 kHz, with no driver. Chapter 25 walks through it.

21 · What dungeon synth is

Slow keyboard music for imagined castles, forests and crypts, usually made by one person at home.

Dungeon synth grew out of the early-1990s black-metal scene, from the synthesizer intros and side projects of musicians such as Mortiis, and was given its name later. It sounds medieval or fantastical rather than modern: drones, simple modal melodies, choirs, horns, harpsichords, all drowned in reverb, often on cheap keyboards and recorded onto cassette. The roughness is part of the charm.

What makes it sound like dungeon synth

  • Slow. 50–90 BPM, long notes, room to breathe.
  • Minor and modal. Natural minor, Dorian, Phrygian, harmonic minor (chapter 23).
  • Drones. One low note held under everything: the GURDY drones, PEDAL, LOW DRONE.
  • Few, simple parts. A pad, a melody, a bass or drone, perhaps a drum. Repetition is a feature: let a phrase come round many times.
  • Huge reverb. HALL with a big SIZE. The space is an instrument.
  • Lo-fi. TAPE wobble and hiss, a little CRSH. It should sound found, not produced.
  • Old-world instruments. Organs, harps, recorders, horns, choirs, bells, the hurdy-gurdy: the sounds GHOULBOX is built around.

Its neighbours

Dungeon synth borders dark ambient (no melody, only atmosphere), winter synth (colder, more echo), comfy synth (cosy, major-key, pastoral) and old video-game music (try LOFI). Any of them can be made on GHOULBOX.

The way in is to listen. Pick an album you love, play along with it on the FM-1 in WHITE mode, and notice how few notes it really uses.

22 · Your first piece, step by step

A complete piece in four parts and two sections, from empty tracks to a song. Take your time: it's an hour's work the first time, and ten minutes after that.

1. Clear the table

  1. Switch the FM-1 on (the power-on scene loads). Don't press PLAY yet.
  2. For each track, T1–T4 (ALGORITHM): go to SEQ › STEP, hold REC and press OCT+. The steps are cleared; the sounds stay.
  3. Turn SELECT to about 66 BPM.

2. The drone (track 4)

  1. Choose T4. Its sound is DRONE WHEEL, a hurdy-gurdy drone. On EDIT, check DRN is D.
  2. Go to SEQ › PHRASES, choose PEDAL and press OCT+. Press PLAY: a low D that never stops. Leave it playing.

3. The chords (track 2)

  1. Choose T2 (CRYPT PAD). On SCL: ROOT D, SCL MIN, QNT WHITE.
  2. SEQ › PATTERN: LEN 4, DIV 1/1. Four steps, a bar each.
  3. SEQ › STEP, cursor on step 1 (KNOB 1). Press and release each chord shape in turn; each fills one step:
    • C + E + G (D minor)
    • A + C + E (B♭ major)
    • B + D + F (C major)
    • C + E + G (D minor again)
  4. The pad now plays Dm – B♭ – C – Dm, one of the oldest progressions in the genre, over the drone.

4. The melody (track 1)

  1. Choose T1 (HURDY GURDY). On SCL: ROOT D, SCL MIN, QNT WHITE. On EDIT, BUZZ to 0 for now: a plain, sad gurdy.
  2. SEQ › PATTERN: LEN 32, DIV 1/8 (four bars, the same length as the chords).
  3. Play along with the chords, slowly, until a few notes feel right. Simple is good: a handful of notes, mostly stepping to neighbours, ending on the C key (D).
  4. Press REC, wait for the loop to come round, and play it. Press REC again when you're done. Not right? Hold REC on STEP to clear (OCT+), and try again.

Rather not play it live? Load LAMENT or BALLAD from PHRASES instead, or write it a note at a time on STEP (chapter 17).

5. The drum (track 3)

  1. Choose T3. Turn PRESETS to TOMB DRUMS. Load TOMBBEAT from PHRASES.
  2. Too busy? On SEQ › CHANCE, give a few steps 50%: the beat thins out and never repeats exactly.

6. Mix and room

  1. GLO › MIXER: bring the drone and drum down until the melody and chords sit on top.
  2. FX: give the pad and drum plenty of REV, the melody a little less. FX › REVERB: SIZE high. FX › TAPE: TAPE 60.

7. Save it, then make section B

  1. Stop. Save as project 1 (chapter 18). Call it VERSE.
  2. On T2, go to STEP, move the cursor back to step 1 (KNOB 1) and play new chords over the old ones: Gm – Dm – A – A: keys F + A + C, C + E + G, and for A major, set SCL to HARM first, then G + B + D twice. (Harmonic minor turns the fifth chord major: drama.)
  3. On T1, turn BUZZ up and COUP to 1/8, and record a new melody, or mute it for a quieter middle.
  4. Save as project 2, BRIDGE.

8. The song

  1. Hold SEQ. Make the rows: A ×2, B ×1, A ×2.
  2. Press PLAY on the SONG page. That's a piece.
The song plays the steps of each section with the sounds loaded right now, so the BUZZ you added in section B stays on when A comes back. Use the sounds' levels and the mutes on the MIXER rather than sound changes to make sections differ, or set the sound back before saving each section.

23 · Minor keys and chords

A cheat sheet. You need no theory beyond this page to write dungeon synth.

Chords in a nutshell

A chord is three or more notes played together. The basic chord, a triad, is built by taking every other note of the scale: the 1st, 3rd and 5th; or the 2nd, 4th and 6th; and so on. Major chords sound bright, minor chords sad.

Shapes on the white keys

With QNT on WHITE, every-other-white-key is always a chord of your scale. The shapes below give the chords of D minor with ROOT D and SCL MIN; change ROOT and the same shapes play the same chords in the new key.

Keys to pressIn D minorDegreeFeel
C · E · GD minor (D F A)iHome
D · F · AE diminished (E G B♭)ii°Tense, unstable: use sparingly
E · G · BF major (F A C)IIIHope, light
F · A · CG minor (G B♭ D)ivDeeper sadness
G · B · DA minor (A C E)vRestless, leading home
A · C · EB♭ major (B♭ D F)VIGrand, epic
B · D · FC major (C E G)VIIMarching, heroic

With SCL HARM (harmonic minor), G · B · D becomes A major (A C♯ E): a strong pull back home. With PHRY, D · F · A becomes E♭ major: ominous.

Progressions that work

One finger, whole chords

On SCL › CHORD, set CHRD to DIA3: each key plays the triad of the scale that starts on it (DIA7 adds a fourth note for a richer sound). In WHITE mode with ROOT D, one finger on the C key plays D minor, on A plays B♭, and so on. VOIC spreads the notes: OPEN for a wide, airy pad, +OCT to double the bass an octave down.

Melody tips

  • Start and end on the root. Lean on the notes of the current chord on its strong beats.
  • Move mostly by step (to neighbouring white keys); save leaps for moments.
  • Repeat a phrase, then change only its ending.
  • Let the melody rest. Silence in a big reverb is music too.

24 · Dungeon recipes

Starting points. Load the sound with PRESETS, then adjust.

Old Tower-style pad

CRYPT PAD or DARK STRINGS on a track, the DIRGE pattern from SEQ › PHRASES, REVERB SIZE high, TAPE around 60. Slow everything down: tempo 60–70, DIV 1/4 or 1/2.

Lone hurdy-gurdy

HURDY GURDY with DRN set to your key. Use COUP 1/8 for rhythmic buzz strokes on a dance tune, or BUZZ at zero for a sad, plain drone.

Monastery

MONKS on one track and CRYPT CHOIR an octave above on another, the CHANT pattern, REVERB HALL with SIZE at maximum. Add PIPE ORGAN or REN ORGAN holding the root.

Tavern ballad

FOLK HARP or a lute on an arpeggio (ARP), TENOR RECORD or LOFI FLUTE for the tune, BAGPIPE for a reedy lead over its drones. SCL DOR or MAJ for a lighter mood, a little swing.

War council

FRENCH HORN or WAR HORN playing slow open chords (VOIC OPEN), TUBA or DIRGE BASS on MARCH, TOMB DRUMS on TOMBBEAT. SCL HARM.

The cave

A NOISE sound for dripping water or wind on one track, LOW DRONE on another, nothing else. DLY with long TIME and high FDBK on the noise. Dark ambient in two tracks.

8-bit dungeon

LOFI sounds for melody and bass, ARP on UPDN at 1/16 for the chords, ROOM reverb instead of HALL, no tape: an old console's dungeon level.

Lo-fi cassette demo

Any of the above with TAPE 80–100 and CRSH 30–50: the sound of a 1990s dungeon-synth tape.

25 · Recording and sharing

The FM-1 is its own audio interface: one USB-C cable carries your music to a computer, with no extra hardware.

Into a computer

  1. Hold HOME for the menu. On AUDIO, set SPEAKER EQ to FLAT (the other settings colour the recording). For a level that doesn't depend on the MASTER knob, set USB LEVEL to FIXED.
  2. Connect the FM-1 with a USB data cable. It appears as an audio input named "Felucca", stereo, 44.1 kHz. No driver is needed on Windows, macOS or Linux. (On a Mac with macOS 13–15, MENU › SYSTEM › USB SERIAL must be OFF; it is, from 1.2 on.)
  3. In recording software (Audacity is free; any DAW works), choose "Felucca" as the input, stereo, and record while the song plays.
  4. Watch the level meter: the loudest moments should stay below the top. If it hits the top, turn the track levels down on GLO › MIXER, or MASTER if USB LEVEL is on MASTER.

Into a recorder or a mixer

Use the 3.5 mm audio out with a stereo cable. Turn MASTER to about three-quarters and set the level on the receiving end.

Finishing

  • Trim the silence at the start and end, and let the last reverb tail ring out fully before cutting.
  • Fade in and out if the piece starts or ends abruptly.
  • Export as WAV for keeping, MP3 for sharing.

Sharing

Dungeon synth lives on Bandcamp, YouTube, SoundCloud and small cassette labels. Tag it "dungeon synth" and the instrument (FM-1, GHOULBOX); people do look. If you'd like the GHOULBOX community to hear it, post a link in the project's GitHub discussions or wherever you found GHOULBOX.

The installer can't find the FM-1Use a USB data cable, close other music software, use Chrome or Edge.
The installer stops after "update mode"Unplug and replug the USB cable and press Install again. The FM-1 waits in update mode until finished.
"NO SAMPLE" and a plain toneThe SAMPLE or GRAIN source is an empty user slot (USR1–3). Pick a built-in set or upload a sample in the web editor.
A keyboard or DAW plays the wrong trackGLO › SYSTEM › ROUT: CH1-4 sends channels 1–4 to tracks 1–4 and ignores 5–16; SEL sends every channel to the selected track.
A keyboard on MIDI IN plays nothingTry the other type of 3.5 mm MIDI adapter (Type A or B), and check the keyboard sends on channel 1–4 (or set ROUT to SEL).
No USB audio on a MacMENU › SYSTEM › USB SERIAL OFF (macOS 13–15).
The recording sounds thin or boomyMENU › AUDIO › SPEAKER EQ to FLAT.
The screen is black after an interrupted updateIf the computer sees a device named WL80UBOOT, the FM-1 is in its boot mode and can be recovered: try another cable and run the installer again. Felucca's README describes further recovery.
Something elseReport it at github.com/jasonpersinger/ghoulbox-fm1-dungeon-synth/issues with your OS, browser and the installer's log text.

27 · Glossary

Accent
A step played louder than the others (FLAG ACC).
ADSR
Attack, decay, sustain, release: the four stages of an envelope (chapter 9).
Arpeggiator
Plays a held chord one note at a time, in time with the tempo (ARP).
Attack
How long a note takes to reach full loudness.
Automation
Knob moves recorded into the sequence so they happen by themselves.
Bar
A group of four beats; music is counted in bars.
BPM
Beats per minute: the tempo.
Cent
A hundredth of a semitone; used for fine tuning (TUNE).
Chord
Three or more notes played together.
Chorus
An effect that adds a wavering copy of the sound, making it wider.
Cutoff
The point above which a low-pass filter removes the sound's brightness (CUT).
DAW
Digital audio workstation: music software for recording and arranging.
Decay
How long a note takes to fall from its peak to the sustain level.
Delay
An effect that repeats the sound, each repeat quieter.
Detune
Putting two oscillators or voices slightly out of tune with each other, for width.
Drone
A long, unchanging note held under the music.
Engine
One of GHOULBOX's 13 ways of making sound.
Envelope
A shape over time, started by each note, that moves loudness (and more).
Filter
Removes part of a sound's frequencies; changes its tone.
FM
Frequency modulation: oscillators changing each other's pitch very fast, making complex tones.
Formant
The resonances that make a voice sound like one vowel or another (VOICE).
Gate
How long a note sounds within its step.
Glide
A slide in pitch from one note to the next (also portamento).
Granular
Making sound from many tiny overlapping slices of a recording (GRAIN).
Legato
Notes joined smoothly, without starting again.
LFO
Low-frequency oscillator: a slow, inaudible wave that moves other controls.
MIDI
The language instruments use to send notes and control moves to each other.
MIDI CC
A control change: a MIDI message for a knob or slider, numbered 0–127.
Mode (scale)
One of the old scales: Dorian, Phrygian and others.
Modulation
One thing moving another: an LFO moving pitch, an envelope moving the filter.
Mono / poly
One note at a time / several notes at once.
Octave
The distance from a note to the next of the same name: twice the pitch.
Oscillator
The part that makes the raw sound by repeating a waveform.
Overdub
Recording more on top of what is already recorded.
Pad
A soft, sustained background sound, usually chords.
Pan
Where a sound sits between left and right.
Pattern
A track's row of steps.
Preset
A ready-made sound (also: patch).
Progression
A sequence of chords.
Project
A saved session: all four tracks, sounds, steps and settings.
Quantize
Moving notes onto a grid: of pitch (a scale) or of time (steps).
Ratchet
A step played several times quickly within its length.
Release
How long a note rings after the key is let go.
Resonance
A filter ringing at its cutoff, adding a whistling edge (RES).
Reverb
The blurred echoes of a room.
Root
The home note of a key or chord.
Scale
A set of notes that belong together.
Semitone
The smallest step on the keyboard: one key to the very next.
Send
How much of a track goes into a shared effect.
Sequencer
Plays stored notes in time, round and round.
Step
One box of time in the sequencer.
Sustain
The loudness a note holds at while the key stays down.
Swing
Delaying every second step a little, for a lilt.
Tie
A step that holds the previous note on instead of playing a new one.
Timbre
The tone or colour of a sound.
Track
One of the four parts, each with its own sound and steps.
Transpose
Moving notes up or down by a number of semitones.
Triad
A three-note chord built from every other note of a scale.
Trompette
The hurdy-gurdy's buzzing string (BUZZ).
Unison
One note played by several detuned voices at once.
Velocity
How hard a key is struck (from a MIDI keyboard).
Voice
One sounding note; GHOULBOX has 8 shared by the tracks.
Waveform
The shape an oscillator repeats: sine, triangle, square, saw.
Wow and flutter
The slow and fast pitch wavering of old tape (TAPE).
